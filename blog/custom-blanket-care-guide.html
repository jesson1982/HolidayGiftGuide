Custom Blanket Care Guide

Updated: January 8, 2026 | 16 min read | By Emma Thompson

Short answer: The simplest way to keep a custom printed blanket looking fresh is to wash it cold on a gentle cycle, skip the bleach, and dry it on low heat or by air. Most fleece and knit blankets hold up for years when you avoid high heat, fabric softeners, and rough washing. Turn the blanket inside out before the first wash to protect the print, and keep it out of direct sunlight during storage. If pilling appears, a fabric shaver restores the surface, and a cool iron on the reverse side smooths creases without harming the ink.

Bottom line: Turn the blanket inside out and wash it cold before the first use, and the print will stay crisp for years of regular cuddling.

Why the first wash sets the tone

Printed blankets leave the factory with ink that is still settling into the fibers. The first wash is when loose dye and surface residue come off, and the way you handle that load decides how the colors behave afterward. A cold, gentle cycle locks the print in place, while hot water can loosen the bond between ink and fabric. We have tested this on our own orders and the difference between a careful first wash and a hot one shows up within just a few cycles of use.

Curing also matters for long-term brightness. Sublimation and dye-based prints need the fibers to relax, and a mild detergent lets that happen without stripping color. Avoid bleach and brighteners on the first wash especially, because they attack the print at its weakest point. Think of the first wash as a setup step rather than just cleaning, and the rest of the blanket's life gets noticeably easier from there.

A common mistake is washing a new blanket with towels or jeans. The heavy weave of those items scrubs the printed face and lifts color before the ink has fully cured. Keep the first load small, cool, and gentle, and the panel will thank you with sharper detail when the blanket finally reaches the couch or the bed where it will live.

Washing temperature and cycle

Cold water is the safe default for every printed blanket we carry. Set the machine to 30 C or lower, choose the gentle or delicate cycle, and use a liquid detergent rather than powder. Powder can leave grit that scratches the surface during the spin. Turn the blanket inside out so the printed face rubs against itself instead of against zippers and other laundry in the drum.

Wash these blankets with similar colors and avoid heavy items like denim that would abrade the print. If your blanket is a photo design with fine detail, a mesh laundry bag adds a second layer of protection. For households with hard water, a splash of white vinegar in the rinse helps remove mineral film that can dull a print over months. It also neutralizes any leftover detergent so the blanket feels softer. This small step costs almost nothing and is one of the few additives we actually recommend for printed fleece care at home.

Drying without heat damage

High heat is the fastest way to fade a print and shrink a blanket. Tumble dry on the lowest setting only if the care tag allows it, and pull the blanket out while it is still slightly damp to finish on a rack. Air drying is safer for photo panels, where the image sits closer to the surface and reacts poorly to hot air.

If you must use a dryer, add dryer balls instead of softener sheets, which leave a film that dulls colors and traps lint. Weighted blankets should always air dry because the glass or plastic filling can shift in heat and create lumpy spots. A flat rack keeps the shape even and stops the print from creasing along hard fold lines that later show as shadows.

Line drying outdoors sounds natural but direct sun is the enemy of dye. Hang the blankets in shade or indoors where air moves freely. A breezy bathroom or laundry room does the job without the UV that quietly bleaches a favorite design. Give the blanket a gentle shake before hanging so it dries smooth instead of with set-in wrinkles.

Fixing pilling on fleece

Pilling is normal on fleece after weeks of use, and it is not a sign the blanket is failing. The raised balls are loose fibers tangling on the surface, and they come off with the right tool. A light once-over restores the smooth hand feel without hurting the print underneath, so do not rush to replace a blanket that has simply gone a little fuzzy.

Work in good light and keep the shaver moving so you do not thin one spot. After de-pilling, a cool wash inside out resets the surface and removes the cut fuzz. The routine below covers the steps we use on our own samples before photographing them for the catalog, and it keeps a well-loved blanket presentable for gift-giving.

Some people reach for a lint roller first, but that only moves the fuzz around. A proper shaver cuts the pills at the base, which is what actually returns the fleece to its original feel. Expect to repeat the process every few months on a heavily used blanket, and treat it as routine maintenance rather than a repair.

  • Run a battery fabric shaver on low, moving in one direction across the panel.
  • Wash inside out with like colors to slow new lint from forming.
  • Use liquid detergent instead of powder, which roughens fibers faster.
  • Skip the dryer sheet and avoid overdrying, since dry heat balls fibers.
  • Brush the surface with a clean clothes brush after each wash to lift fuzz.

Storing between seasons

Off-season storage is where prints quietly fade. Keep blankets in a cool, dry closet away from windows, because sunlight breaks down both dye and fiber over months of exposure. A breathable cotton bag beats a plastic bin, which traps moisture and can spot the print with condensation that you will not notice until spring.

Fold loosely rather than compressing the blanket into a tight cube, and never store it damp. A cedar block or lavender sachet keeps moths away without the chemical film that mothballs leave on fabric. Check stored blankets once mid-season so any moisture issue is caught early, before it spreads across the printed area and ruins the design.

If you live somewhere humid, a small silica packet tucked into the storage bag helps. Just keep it away from the print face so it cannot leave a mark. Label the bag with the recipient and occasion too, because a stored gift is easy to forget until the next holiday is suddenly two weeks away.

Reviving tired fleece

Fleece that has gone flat or matted can often be brought back. A short cold wash with a half cup of white vinegar in the rinse softens the fibers and removes the film that makes fleece feel stiff. Skip fabric softener, which coats the fibers and traps odor over time instead of solving the problem.

For a flat, pressed look on display blankets, use a cool iron on the reverse side with a thin cloth between. Never iron the printed face directly, because the heat can smear the ink. A ten-minute tumble with clean tennis balls on air fluff also lifts the nap back up without heat, which is handy before a blanket comes out of the closet for guests.

A steamer held at a distance can refresh a wrinkled throw without touching the surface. Keep the nozzle moving and never let water pool on the print. These small revival tricks extend the life of a personalized gift well past the point where a cheaper blanket would have been retired to the back of a shelf.

Knowing when to retire a blanket

Even good care has limits. If the print has cracked across a large area, the fabric has thinned to see-through, or the blanket no longer holds warmth, it has earned retirement. These signs usually appear after years, not months, when care is consistent from the start of ownership.

Before you retire it, consider cutting a clean panel out as a keepsake or photo backup, since many customers lose the original image file. A well-cared blanket from our premium fleece blanket line often lasts five years of regular use, which is a fair return on a personalized gift that started as a simple idea and a single photo.

If the blanket was a memorial or anniversary piece, retiring the physical item does not mean losing the memory. Scan the remaining print at high resolution and keep the file with your other photos. That way the design lives on even after the fabric has done its job, and a replacement can be reprinted as a fresh custom blanket gift idea from the same source later if you want one.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can I put a custom blanket in the washing machine?

Yes. Wash it cold on a gentle cycle with liquid detergent, turned inside out. Avoid bleach, fabric softener, and powder detergent. A mesh bag adds protection for photo panels. Machine washing is safe as long as you keep the temperature at or below 30 C and skip the hot settings that loosen ink from the fibers over time.

How do I stop the colors from fading?

Keep washes cold, dry on low or by air, and store the blanket away from direct sunlight. Sunlight and high heat are the two biggest causes of fade. A half cup of white vinegar in the rinse removes film that dulls prints, and washing inside out protects the face from abrasion. Consistent cool care keeps colors bright for several years of normal use.

What is the best way to remove pilling?

Use a battery fabric shaver on low, moving in one direction across the panel in good light. Wash inside out with like colors afterward to clear the cut fuzz, and brush the surface with a clean clothes brush. Avoid overdrying, which tightens and balls fibers. Pilling is normal wear and comes off cleanly without damaging the print underneath when done carefully.

Can I iron a custom blanket?

Iron only on the reverse side, on a cool setting, with a thin cloth between the iron and the fabric. Never press the printed face directly because heat can smear the ink. For display blankets a steamer held at a distance refreshes wrinkles without contact. Air fluff in the dryer with tennis balls also lifts the nap if you prefer not to iron at all.

How should I store a blanket I only use in winter?

Fold it loosely in a breathable cotton bag, in a cool, dry, dark closet. Add cedar or lavender for moths and a silica packet in humid homes, but keep these away from the print face. Never store it damp or compressed. Check once mid-season so moisture or pests are caught early, before they can mark or fade the design.
