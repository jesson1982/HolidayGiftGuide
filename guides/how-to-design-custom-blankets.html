How to Design Custom Blankets: A Complete Step-by-Step Guide

Updated: January 15, 2026 | 15 min read | By Holiday Gift Guide Team

Designing custom blankets is an art form that combines creativity with technical precision. Whether you're creating a personalized gift, launching a product line, or preserving family memories, understanding the design process ensures your final product looks professional and prints perfectly. This comprehensive guide will walk you through every step of designing custom blankets, from concept to final file preparation.

Understanding Blanket Types and Their Design Requirements

Before diving into the design process, it's crucial to understand that different blanket materials have different printing characteristics and design requirements. The three main types of custom blankets are:

Fleece Blankets

Fleece blankets are the most popular choice for custom designs due to their softness, affordability, and excellent print reproduction. Fleece printing typically uses dye-sublimation, which allows for full-color, edge-to-edge printing with vibrant results. Designs for fleece should be created at 300 DPI and can include photographic elements, gradients, and complex color schemes.

Woven Blankets

Woven blankets use a different manufacturing process where threads are actually woven together to create the design. This limits color options (typically 8-12 colors) and requires designs with clear, distinct elements. Photographic images don't work well on woven blankets – instead, opt for bold graphics, text, and simple illustrations with solid colors.

Weighted Blankets

Weighted blankets combine therapeutic benefits with personalization. The design process is similar to fleece blankets, but you must consider that the weight beads inside can affect how the fabric drapes and how the design appears when the blanket is in use. Avoid placing critical design elements where they might be distorted by the weight distribution.

Step 1: Choose Your Blanket Type and Size

Select the appropriate blanket material and size based on your intended use. Consider the following factors:

  • Intended Use: Is this for bedding, decorative purposes, or outdoor use?
  • Target Audience: Children, adults, or pets?
  • Design Complexity: Does your design require full-color printing or work with limited colors?
  • Budget: Fleece is typically the most affordable option

Common blanket sizes include:

  • Small (30x40 inches): Perfect for babies, pets, or decorative throws
  • Medium (50x60 inches): Ideal for adult use on couches and chairs
  • Large (60x80 inches): Great for beds or wrapping around multiple people

Step 2: Select Your Design Software

Choose design software that suits your skill level and design needs:

Professional Options

  • Adobe Photoshop: Best for photographic designs and complex image manipulation
  • Adobe Illustrator: Ideal for vector graphics, text, and designs that need to scale infinitely
  • CorelDRAW: Alternative vector software with similar capabilities to Illustrator

Beginner-Friendly Options

  • Canva: Free web-based design tool with templates and easy-to-use interface
  • PicMonkey: Simple photo editing and design tool
  • GIMP: Free alternative to Photoshop with robust features

Step 3: Set Up Your Canvas with Correct Dimensions

Proper canvas setup is critical for successful blanket printing. Follow these specifications:

  • Resolution: Set to 300 DPI (dots per inch) for print quality
  • Dimensions: Exact size of your blanket in inches
  • Color Mode: CMYK for accurate color reproduction
  • Bleed: Add 0.5-inch bleed on all sides for printing and cutting variations

For example, if you're designing a 50x60 inch blanket, set your canvas to 51x61 inches to account for the bleed area.

Step 4: Design Your Layout

Plan your layout carefully, considering how the blanket will be used and viewed:

Placement Guidelines

  • Keep important design elements at least 1 inch from the edges
  • Place faces and text in the center third of the blanket when possible
  • Consider how the design will look when the blanket is folded
  • Think about which parts will be most visible when the blanket is in use

Design Tips

  • Use grid guidelines to ensure proper spacing and alignment
  • Avoid placing critical elements where the blanket might fold frequently
  • Consider the blanket's orientation – landscape or portrait
  • Design for the primary viewing angle based on intended use

Step 5: Prepare Your Images and Graphics

High-quality source images are essential for professional results:

Image Requirements

  • Resolution: Minimum 300 DPI at final print size
  • Format: PNG, JPG, or TIFF for raster images; AI, EPS, or SVG for vectors
  • Quality: Check images at 100% zoom to ensure no pixelation
  • Color: Convert to CMYK for accurate color representation

Text Handling

  • Convert all text to outlines or paths before exporting
  • Use fonts that are legible at the blanket's viewing distance
  • Ensure sufficient contrast between text and background
  • Avoid fonts smaller than 24pt for better readability

Step 6: Review and Proof Your Design

Thorough review prevents costly mistakes:

Quality Check

  • Zoom in to 100% to check for pixelation or artifacts
  • Verify all spelling and grammar
  • Check alignment and spacing of all elements
  • Ensure colors appear as intended in CMYK mode

Test Print

  • Print a scaled-down version to check overall composition
  • Print at actual size if possible for critical projects
  • View the print from the expected viewing distance
  • Make adjustments based on the test print results

Step 7: Export in the Correct Format

Proper file export ensures your design prints as intended:

File Format Options

  • PDF: Best overall format – preserves layers, fonts, and quality
  • PNG: Good for designs with transparency
  • JPG: Acceptable for photographic designs, uses compression
  • AI/EPS: Ideal for vector designs that may need editing

Export Settings

  • Maintain 300 DPI resolution
  • Use appropriate compression settings
  • Ensure file size is within upload limits (typically under 25MB)
  • Include bleed area in the exported file

Step 8: Upload and Preview

The final step before production:

  • Upload your design file using the provider's interface
  • Use the preview tool to verify placement and quality
  • Check that the design fits within the printable area
  • Make any final adjustments before placing your order
  • Consider ordering a sample before bulk production

Common Design Mistakes to Avoid

  • Low Resolution: Using images below 300 DPI results in pixelated prints
  • RGB Color Mode: Can cause color shifts between screen and print
  • Ignoring Bleed: Designs too close to edges may get cut off
  • Small Text: Text that's too small becomes unreadable on blankets
  • Poor Contrast: Insufficient contrast makes designs hard to see

Professional Tips for Stunning Results

  • Use high-quality source images for the best print results
  • Design with the blanket's texture in mind – some materials show more detail than others
  • Consider how the blanket will be used and viewed in the final context
  • Order a sample before large production runs to verify quality
  • Keep backup copies of your design files in native format

By following this comprehensive guide, you'll be well-equipped to create professional-quality custom blanket designs that print beautifully and exceed expectations. Remember that practice makes perfect – don't be afraid to experiment and refine your design process with each project.

Frequently Asked Questions

What resolution should my blanket design be?

Your blanket design should be at 300 DPI (dots per inch) resolution at the final print size. This ensures crisp, clear printing without pixelation or blurring. Lower resolutions (72 DPI or 150 DPI) may look fine on screen but will print poorly, appearing fuzzy or pixelated. Always design at print resolution from the start to avoid quality issues.

What file format is best for blanket printing?

PDF is generally the best format as it preserves layers, fonts, and formatting while maintaining high quality. PNG is excellent for designs with transparency requirements, while JPG works well for photographic designs. Vector files (AI, EPS, SVG) are ideal for designs with text and simple graphics as they can be scaled infinitely without quality loss. Check with your specific printer for their preferred format requirements.

How do I ensure colors print accurately?

Design in CMYK color mode rather than RGB to see how colors will appear in print. Use a calibrated monitor and consider investing in color calibration tools for critical color work. Request physical proofs for projects where exact color matching is essential. Keep in mind that screen colors will always differ slightly from printed colors due to the different color spaces (RGB for screens, CMYK for print).

Can I use photos from social media for blanket designs?

While you can use social media photos, they're often compressed and low-resolution, which can result in poor print quality. For best results, use original, high-resolution photos whenever possible. If you must use social media images, choose the highest quality version available and be prepared for potential quality limitations. Always ensure you have the rights to use any photos in your designs.

How long does the design-to-production process take?

The timeline varies depending on complexity and provider. Simple designs can be uploaded and produced within 2-3 business days, while complex custom projects may take 1-2 weeks including design time, proofing, and production. Always factor in additional time for shipping, especially during busy holiday seasons. Planning ahead and allowing buffer time ensures your project arrives when needed.
